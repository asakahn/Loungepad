Your own shortcut wheels, the joystick mouse on the Power Wheel, and games started by a launcher
followed all the way in.

## New

- **Shortcuts are yours to arrange.** The Power Wheel's Shortcuts spoke now opens a wheel, like
  Actions, and what is on it is up to you: an app from the Start menu (Store apps included), a game
  from your library, a folder, a file, a website, a command or script, one of Windows' own windows,
  or a power action (lock, rest, sleep, sign out, restart, shut down; the last three always ask
  first). Make as many tabs as you like, up to eight shortcuts on each, and turn between them with
  LB and RB.
- **Settings → Shortcuts.** The new category is the wheel itself, laid out the way the Power Wheel
  draws it. A on a spoke edits it, A on the dashed + adds one, X picks one up so you can slide it
  round the ring or carry it to another tab, and Y opens the options. Up from the top of the ring
  reaches the tabs, where X picks a tab up to move it along. With a mouse, drag a spoke where you
  want it. Any shortcut can also have a button that opens it from the desktop, without the wheel.
  The shortcuts the wheel had before are your first tab.
- **Settings → Actions works the same way.** Each app's actions are a wheel with tabs now, the app's
  own first and Everywhere's after, and LB and RB turn between them on the Power Wheel. Open an app
  in Settings → Actions and you get the same editor as Shortcuts: A edits an action, the dashed +
  adds one, X moves one round the ring or to another tab, and Y opens the options, where you can
  also restore the app's defaults. The long list of actions is gone, and so is "Show on the wheel":
  an action is either on a tab or taken off. One you had kept off the wheel is taken off, unless
  it has a button, which keeps working. Anything taken off can be put back from the + spoke.
- **Bring actions in from elsewhere.** On an app's wheel, Y, then: copy another app's wheel (set up
  Chrome once and give Brave or Thorium the same), open a file of actions somebody saved for you, or
  pick one of the community's layouts for that app. You see the wheel you will get before anything
  changes, choose whether it replaces yours or comes in as new tabs, and buttons come along only if
  you ask. One import can be undone. "Save to a file" in the same options shares yours.
- **Every action says what it does.** A short description sits in the action wheel's centre as you
  point at an action, and on its card in Settings, for anyone still learning the shortcuts. Write
  your own for the actions you add, or change any of them.
- **You can tell whose tab is whose.** The action wheel's tabs each show their app's icon, with the
  globe on Everywhere's, and a line between the app's tabs and Everywhere's.
- **Icons that look like what they open.** Apps, programs, folders and files show their own icons,
  games their art, and websites the site's own icon. Any shortcut can wear a picture or an icon from
  your PC instead.

- **Enable / Disable joystick mouse on the Power Wheel.** The spoke that used to say "Mouse in
  game" now turns the stick-as-a-mouse on or off. Over a game it is the mouse in that game: off
  by default, so the game has the controller to itself, and one press gives you the pointer and
  clicks there until the next launch. Anywhere else it turns the joystick mouse on or off
  everywhere. The label always says what it will do. Settings → Controller calls it Joystick
  mouse too.

## Fixed

- **Games started by a launcher.** Loungepad now follows everything a game it launched starts,
  wherever it lives on your PC. With Prism Launcher, the stick is a mouse over Prism, where you
  pick and launch an instance, and stands down once Minecraft is in front, instead of the other
  way round. Close game, pause, keeping the game on the TV and the Stats readings reach the real
  game too, and the session carries on when a launcher closes itself after starting it. The same
  goes for other launchers: the Minecraft Launcher, MultiMC, CurseForge, Modrinth, REDlauncher,
  the Paradox launcher and Bethesda's game launchers are known by name, and any other launcher
  is told apart once its game's window opens. A browser, a store client or Notepad that a game
  opens is never taken for the game.

## Download and install

1. Under **Assets** below, download **Loungepad.exe**. That one file is the whole app.
2. Put it anywhere you like, such as your Desktop, and double-click it.
3. If Windows says it protected your PC, click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

Already using Loungepad? It updates itself, so there is nothing to download. You can ignore the
other files: Loungepad fetches the input service itself when you turn it on, and the
`-win-x64.zip` is only for copies on version 1.6.3 or older.
